# Mail organization and retention

Version 0.7.1 uses classifier version 3 with prompt/schema version 2. The provider transport accepts an exact whole-response bare or JSON code fence and correctly typed empty optional tool metadata. It still rejects extra prose, nonempty/malformed tool metadata, tool/permission events and invalid response schemas. Failed results receive a bounded retry budget for the new classifier; successful cached classifications are preserved.

MailHarbor's optional organizer discovers mail across connected accounts, marks messages read, saves one classification per logical message, applies shared labels, and checks when an archive or Trash action becomes due. It runs on the homeserver after the browser closes. It uses the separately configured Gemini worker; ordinary browsing and local invoice extraction do not require a model call.

Automatic processing requires the owner's permission to send bounded message text to Google through Agy. This scope includes historical mail, custom folders, Sent, Drafts, Junk and Trash. Attachments and encrypted message bodies are excluded from model input. Configure tender grace explicitly before enabling deadline-based tender actions. An unconfigured tender grace holds dated tenders.

## Rules

These are the application's configured policies, rather than an editable rule builder. The Accounts panel shows their current values. “Trash” means move to the provider's Trash folder; MailHarbor never permanently purges messages. Providers may empty Trash automatically, so recovery is subject to the provider's retention period.

| Label | ID | Action | When |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Promotions / Coupons | `coupons` | Trash | Every offer has a confidently established expiry and it has passed. An offer with unknown expiry keeps the message in place. |
| Development / GitHub | `development` | Trash | 1 month after receipt. |
| Social | `social` | Trash | 1 week after receipt. |
| Jobs | `jobs` | Trash | 1 month after receipt. |
| Security / Account Alerts | `security` | Archive | 1 month after receipt. |
| Travel & Events | `travel` | Archive | 2 years after receipt. |
| Work & Administration | `work` | Archive | 1 year after receipt. |
| Newsletters | `newsletters` | Trash | 1 month after receipt. |
| Finance | `finance` | Archive | 2 years after receipt. |
| Invoices & Receipts | `invoices` | Archive | 7 years after receipt, subject to unresolved invoice filing protection. |
| Tenders | `tenders` | Archive | After the configured grace period following a confirmed deadline; hold while grace is unconfigured. Without a confirmed deadline, 6 calendar months after receipt. |
| Appointments | `appointments` | Archive | After the confidently established appointment end. |
| Orders | `orders` | Archive | 7 years after receipt. |

Sent mail has a 7-year archive rule. Drafts have a 2-month Trash rule independent of labels, provided the message date and complete content can be verified. Existing Trash messages are analyzed and marked read but are not moved or purged. Junk remains in place unless it qualifies for rescue below.

Age starts from the provider's internal received date when available, with the parsed message date as fallback. Invoice **Drive folder routing** continues to use the invoice date; that is separate from the email's retention age. Invalid, missing or future receipt dates prevent automatic moves.

Months and years use calendar arithmetic in `Europe/Brussels`, including month-end clamping: one month after 31 January is the last day of February at the corresponding local time. A week is seven local calendar days. Offset-qualified event timestamps retain their instant; a date-only expiry remains valid through that local date. During the autumn clock change the later matching time is used, and nonexistent spring times move forward.

When labels overlap, the latest retention deadline wins. If any applicable rule archives, the final action is Archive. For example, an invoice included in a newsletter remains protected by the 7-year invoice rule and will be archived. An uncertain required expiry also holds the whole message. Manual labels added by the owner participate in this protection. Removing an automatic label hides it from label views; it does **not** erase the saved classification or cancel its retention rule. Pause processing to stop automatic actions.

## Review and Junk rescue

The model returns category, confidence, spam assessment, and optional event dates. It cannot return a mailbox action. Requests sent to Gemini use short batch-local IDs and omit unknown date fields. In 0.7.0, classifier/prompt/schema version 2 shares field names, date syntax and limits between the prompt and validator. A wrapper restores the full message IDs and explicit null date fields before full validation. Each requested ID must occur exactly once; extra IDs, unsupported categories, invalid probabilities, impossible dates and inconsistent appointment ranges are rejected. Explicit-zone timestamps precise to a minute normalize to zero seconds. Valid numeric date confidence with no dates normalizes to zero; missing evidence for an actual date is still rejected. Existing successful version-1 classifications remain valid.

Automatic category retention requires classification confidence of at least `0.90`. Date-dependent moves require date confidence of at least `0.95`. Incomplete, truncated, encrypted or unavailable content, ambiguous dates, stale message identities and unresolved invoice filing records keep mail in place. Accounts → Mail organization separates **Your decisions**, **Automatic holds**, **Technical failures** and **Scheduled retries**, with all recorded blocker reasons. Missing promotion expiry is an automatic hold. Invoice filing in progress is waiting; a concrete invoice review decision remains in Your decisions. These categories do not change the retention protections.

The review list can show a verified plain-text message preview, queue another analysis attempt, save **Keep**, or confirm category/date evidence. Keep persists across restart and prevents retention actions. Confirmation requires complete, freshly verified content and preserves existing protective categories; it cannot remove an existing invoice or other category to shorten retention. Owner edits exclude concurrent processor/review writes, and an intervening Pause remains effective. A retry explicitly resets the relevant attempt counters and runs only while processing is enabled. This is not a custom-rule editor.

Temporary body-read failures (`mailbox_error` or `mailbox_timeout`), including a failed whole batch, retry locally no earlier than fifteen minutes later; account cooldown may extend that wait. After three failed attempts, the message remains a technical failure without another automatic body retry. Stale references are technical failures immediately. The budget and deadline survive restart. Normally a cached classification avoids another body read; an explicit retry or migrated incomplete result can request content recovery while its old protective classification is retained.

An invalid multi-message model reply accepts no partial results and reduces the batch to isolate the failure. An invalid single-message reply, timeout or provider error saves a diagnostic and a durable per-message failed-attempt count. The first two failures become eligible again after fifteen and thirty minutes; the third remains a technical failure until a recorded retry or applicable migration. Healthy work continues between retries. Splitting an unsuccessful batch and interrupted requests can create additional provider calls; the three-attempt limit is the isolated-message failure budget, not a promise of exactly three total provider requests.

Three consecutive isolated-message failures open a provider circuit breaker instead of permanently disabling processing. After a fifteen-minute cooldown, one synthetic message probes recovery without using another backlog message. Repeated failed probes increase the cooldown up to four hours. A valid probe resumes a batch size of one; valid mailbox classifications reset the failure streak. Authentication, configuration and storage faults still block processing pending correction. Provider quota uses a shared durable deadline. Explicit validated retry/reset hints take precedence, bounded to one minute through seven days. Without a hint, explicit quota exhaustion waits five hours, a weekly signal waits seven days, and a request-rate/429-only throttle waits one minute. A shorter hint cannot shorten an existing longer deadline; restart does not clear it.

Junk rescue requires complete content and a Gemini verdict of legitimate with confidence at least `0.98`. It applies to both familiar and new senders; previous correspondence is not required. Uncertain assessments and incomplete content stay in Junk. MailHarbor does not train the provider's spam filter or create a provider allowlist.

Rescue uses the verified Inbox as destination, preserving the message and marking it read. Its cached classification then applies normal category retention on a later pass. Other Junk does not inherit deletion rules from its category while it remains in Junk.

## Provider actions

Every flag or move operation revalidates the connected account revision, mailbox identity, exact folder, UIDVALIDITY, UID and immutable message fingerprint. The browser and model never supply raw provider destinations. Read flags are grouped into bounded operations. Messages already marked read are skipped.

Apply mode first handles a bounded page budget of cached unread locations and new headers, then interleaves further discovery, due retention and classification. A large rescan cannot indefinitely exclude classification. Each page's headers are saved before read flags are applied. A mailbox error or timeout saves failed flag work with a retry deadline before advancing discovery; cancellation leaves the page checkpoint unchanged. TLS failures, mailbox authentication errors and account timeouts also receive an account cooldown with exponential backoff and jitter, up to four hours plus jitter. A failed body-read batch advances the account backoff once, while individual messages retain their own read-attempt budgets. Verified successful mailbox operations reset an expired connection-failure streak, so isolated errors do not accumulate into permanent four-hour delays. An active longer deadline remains intact. Authentication errors defer the affected account without consuming message read-attempt budgets or pausing healthy accounts. Affected messages cannot move until their read flags are verified. Cached flag work is revisited at startup and during periodic discovery without repeating saved classifications. Preview mode never changes read flags.

Trash requires an unambiguous provider Trash destination. For non-Gmail accounts, Archive requires an existing configured or verified archive destination. The processor uses native IMAP MOVE; it does not fall back to COPY plus EXPUNGE and does not create guessed folders. Missing capabilities or ambiguous destinations leave the message in place with an explicit outcome.

For Gmail, Archive removes only Inbox membership. It preserves other labels and the message remains in All Mail. Gmail's Sent label cannot be removed by this workflow: sent mail already outside Inbox is already archived in Gmail's model and remains visible in Sent. MailHarbor does not delete sent messages to hide them from Sent.

Locations share one physical move only when the provider is explicitly identified as Gmail and they have the same valid Gmail `emailId` in that account. All matching folder roles participate in the decision, so an Inbox alias cannot bypass the longer Sent rule or another hold. Distinct Gmail IDs, missing Gmail identity and non-Gmail copies remain separate move targets even when their fingerprint shares a classification. A confirmed `applied` or `already_target` result checkpoints all matching aliases; an absent source is not proof of success and permits a later attempt from another remaining alias.

Move intent is persisted before the provider operation. Results, resolved destination references and counters are persisted afterward. A stale or missing source is checked rather than blindly replayed. If a move succeeds but a destination reference cannot be resolved, the app retains that outcome; it does not assume that repeating the old move is safe. Account changes and pause/close signals stop subsequent effects.

A mailbox error, timeout or authentication failure during a move defers unfinished actions for at least fifteen minutes, extended by the affected account's cooldown, while preserving their intents and completed results. Other accounts and pending classifications continue. The next attempt still verifies the source identity; an absent source is not treated as proof of a successful move. Cancellation, account-revision changes and other integrity failures still stop the pass.

## Efficient processing and storage

The organizer incrementally scans selectable provider folders using bounded UID ranges and batches of headers. Each folder pass freezes an upper UID watermark; that bound, UIDVALIDITY and the after-UID checkpoint survive restarts. An unfinished pass resumes its original bound, with later arrivals picked up on the next pass. During a long classification backlog, discovery and read-flag batches run again every fifteen minutes between classification operations, then cached/pending work resumes. A changed UIDVALIDITY restarts discovery rather than trusting old UIDs. Gmail headers written before physical IDs were stored receive a one-time metadata refresh while keeping their cached classifications. Matching account identity and immutable fingerprint share classification work; physical move grouping uses the stricter Gmail identity rule above.

The key is a SHA-256 digest of account ID, normalized mailbox email and message fingerprint. Classification results are saved before labels or moves, together with their classifier version and future retention time; read flags are handled earlier without AI. A successful result is reused across folder observations, retention passes and process restarts. This is not a guarantee of exactly one provider request: an interrupted or unsuccessful model call may need another attempt before its result can be saved.

Automatic classification starts with batches of 12 messages. The controller shrinks unsuccessful or timed-out batches and grows after three full valid batches taking less than ninety seconds, within a maximum of 12. Partial batches do not establish capacity to grow. Scheduling rotates connected accounts and reserves opportunities for due recovery, old mail and newer arrivals. This is an adaptive controller, not a measured optimal batch-size recommendation. The protocol still permits at most 40 messages and 512 KiB of JSON, with at most 8,000 plain-text body characters per message plus bounded metadata.

Category synchronization shares exclusive mailbox writes with the organizer. Label jobs blocked by an organizer move intent or invoice filing defer before acquiring that writer. Productive label work drains the active processing unit, including its prepared body reads and classification, then returns control to the next organizer stage. Inventory position, completed folder scans and the discovery timer survive these cooperative yields in memory. A process restart still resumes the durable folder/message checkpoints. This prevents frequent label polling from restarting inventory and the initial discovery budget indefinitely; owner Pause, account identity checks and provider cooldowns still stop dependent work.

Body extraction version 2 records fixed completeness reasons such as unavailable part, fetch failure, encoded/decoded limit, encryption and unsupported content. Processing starts with the normal 64-KiB encoded prefix. If that source limit alone prevents recovery of usable inline text, it may fetch once more up to 256 KiB with renewed identity checks. MIME headers remain capped at 16 KiB and final text at 8,000 characters; attachments, encrypted content and active HTML remain excluded. A longer local prefix never increases the AI body allowance or turns an incomplete excerpt into complete evidence. Ordinary browsing keeps its 64-KiB source limit. No body or attachment is stored in the organizer's durable index.

`mail-index.sqlite` uses WAL and full synchronous commits. Sensitive record payloads are encrypted individually with AES-256-GCM under a key derived from the existing account-store key. Associated data binds each ciphertext to its record kind and key. Searchable label/account tokens are HMAC-derived; work state and due times are indexed for scheduling. The server holds the key required to decrypt data. The SQLite files and account key must be backed up and protected together. Legacy labels are imported once as manual labels; the former 10,000-entry label limit does not apply to the SQLite index.

Cached headers, classifications, manual/excluded/automatic labels, private provider references, folder checkpoints, invoice queue entries and processing settings persist on the homeserver. They are not automatically erased when source mail is archived or moved to Trash. The index is not a mailbox backup and does not restore message bodies. Disconnecting a mailbox does not erase its historical index; a different mailbox identity cannot inherit that mail.

The processor shares the existing single Agy worker with briefings. There is no second worker, paid-overage fallback or automatic model substitution. A timeout reduces the runtime batch size and schedules a sixty-second worker retry unless a longer breaker/quota deadline applies; an individual failed message still respects its own later deadline. The normal interval between completed automatic passes is fifteen minutes. Pausing aborts active work and retains durable progress. Worker states are `running`, `paused`, `retrying`, `blocked`, `idle` and `stalled`. A next-attempt timestamp is exposed only while enabled; disabled previews and historical blocked states do not promise an automatic retry. Progress timestamps and a missing-schedule/overdue-work check expose stalls.

Counts distinguish logical messages from provider actions. `coverage` assigns each indexed message an analysis state such as classified, pending, retrying, failed, kept or disconnected. `counts.analyzed` is derived from classified records; `counts.pending` includes analysis retries. Review categories describe processing blockers and can concern an already analyzed message. `markedRead` and `moved` are cumulative provider effects; several folder locations can belong to one logical message. Do not add analysis, hold/review and action counts together. Connected-account discovery indicators summarize saved folder checkpoints and account cooldowns; a completed pass is not a claim that disconnected accounts or every current provider folder have been fully covered.

`Errors (total)` is cumulative; `lastRun.errorCount` covers only that run. The encrypted settings retain the most recent 50 safe events with timestamp, phase, account ID when known and fixed error codes. Version 0.7.0 adds allowlisted diagnostic stage/reason pairs for stream protocol, JSON parsing, schema, identity, transport, timeout, authentication, configuration and quota failures. Existing allowlisted mailbox protocol/TLS diagnostics remain available. Completed/failed jobs expose batch size, queue wait and latency, with classifier/schema versions for classification jobs; processor status retains recent batch/provider metrics. Raw provider replies, command arguments, credentials, folder names and message content are excluded. Historical failures whose detail was never saved cannot be reconstructed from these new fields.

When CLI initialization validates successfully, job `startupMs` measures spawn through validated initialization and `responseMs` measures initialization through the terminal response or stop. Missing initialization does not report a fabricated timing. `metrics.stages` separately records calls, last duration and accumulated duration for inventory, discovery, body reads, read flags and moves. Version 0.8.6 distinguishes rejected permission events, tool events/steps, nonempty tool metadata/calls and malformed metadata with fixed reason codes; all remain rejected. Historical `stream_tool_attempt` events cannot be retroactively disambiguated. These numeric observations identify bottlenecks; sustained throughput and the preferred batch size still require a live benchmark.

The additive reliability migration preserves successful classifications and existing move journals. Eligible failed classifications and previously incomplete results can be selectively requeued. Migration pages and their cursor commit together, so restart does not reset retry budgets or repeatedly requeue the same failure. The owner's paused state is preserved. Old protective results remain in place until replacement evidence is validated and committed.

Processing settings record independent classifier, schema, prompt, extractor and policy versions. A migration key makes each applicable version transition resumable. Classification retries are keyed to the combined classifier, schema and prompt versions, so a relevant change can requeue older failed classifications once. Content recovery is keyed separately to the extractor version, allowing one eligible reread after an extraction improvement. A policy change reevaluates cached evidence without an unnecessary AI request. Current-version exhausted failures and attempted content recoveries are not reset simply by restarting.

A renewable SQLite lease belongs to one processor instance from initialization through close. It protects migration, recovery and owner edits as well as runs; duplicate instances cannot both take ownership. Failed durable writes or corrupted encrypted records stop subsequent processor effects. Back up the SQLite database and matching account key together. An older source release may ignore new retry/owner-decision metadata: rollback must keep processing paused and verify state compatibility before resuming. Restoring the database does not undo provider-side moves.

A run that finishes its available work but encounters folder or message-read errors reports `lastRun.status: "partial"`. This is not full-mailbox completion: saved progress remains available and affected work can be retried on a later pass. The Accounts panel distinguishes partial runs from completed, paused and interrupted runs.

## Invoice filing and appointments

When processing applies the Invoices label, it enqueues a bounded header and private reference in the encrypted `invoiceQueue` index. This brings historical invoices from custom folders, Sent and Archive into the existing filing workflow. Identical queued sources and complete ledger sources are not repeatedly written or downloaded.

Each existing 20-minute invoice pass validates the newest-Inbox scan, then merges up to its scan limit of queued historical candidates (normally 1,000) with existing retry sources. A fresh Inbox observation takes precedence over an older queued location. Queued invoices force attachment inspection even when their subject does not mention an invoice. Account revisions are checked, and incomplete scans or capacity failures leave the queue intact. A queued item is removed only after a durable complete, review or retry record exists; a newer concurrent queue entry is retained. The invoice ledger's existing 10,000-record/source capacity remains a separate limit. See [Invoice filing](INVOICES.md) for original-document validation, business VAT routing and Drive deduplication.

An appointment with complete, sufficiently confident start/end information exposes **Add to calendar** in the message reader. The action downloads `appointment.ics` for the user to review and import in their calendar. It does not authorize a Google Calendar connection or create an event automatically. One-day and multi-day all-day events are supported; inclusive input end dates become exclusive ICS end dates. The generated file escapes and folds text, uses a stable event UID, and excludes invitations, attendees, organizers, attachments, alarms and executable links.

## Authenticated API

These routes use the existing exact Host/Origin, owner session, CSRF and no-store rules. Mutations require POST. Calendar download is the narrowly allowed GET query exception and accepts only an opaque message ID.

| Route | Request | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `GET /api/mail/processing/status` | None | Worker state, progress, coverage and reason counts, account discovery/cooldowns, safe diagnostics, batch metrics, migration, policies and preview. |
| `POST /api/mail/processing/settings` | Optional `providerConsent`, tender grace, `maxActions`, `maxClassifications` | Updated status. Rejected during a run or owner edit. |
| `POST /api/mail/processing` | `{ "action": "start" }`, `"pause"` or `"preview"` | HTTP 202 with current status. Start and preview return while the background pass runs. |
| `GET /api/mail/processing/review?category=…&after=…` | Category `review`, `held`, `failed` or `retry`; optional opaque cursor | Up to 50 records, recorded reasons and next cursor. |
| `GET /api/mail/processing/review/message?id=…` | One opaque review-list ID | Verified bounded plain-text preview and completeness information. |
| `POST /api/mail/processing/review` | `{id,action:"retry"|"keep"|"confirm",labels?,dates?}` | Saved resolution and updated status; confirmation validates the supplied evidence. |
| `GET /api/mail/calendar?id=…` | One opaque ID from an authenticated mail listing | `text/calendar` attachment, or a safe error when no verified appointment exists. |

The configured tender rule uses `tenderGraceDays: 0` and `tenderGraceMonths: null`. The settings endpoint also validates the earlier bounded alternatives of two days or two months, but those are not the owner's selected policy. Null/null remains a fail-safe hold for dated tenders on an unconfigured installation; other categories can still operate. This endpoint is not a general policy-editing API. `providerConsent` must be true before a model-backed start or preview.

A preview uses cached results and may classify a bounded sample of previously unseen mail. It estimates due actions without changing provider flags, moving mail, adding labels or filing invoices. The first preview is a sample, not a forecast for the entire mailbox. Its classifications and progress are saved for reuse. Check `running`, `mode` and `lastRun.status` before treating preview counts as final.

Pilot settings accept `maxActions: 0..1000` and `maxClassifications: 1..1000`, or `null` to remove a cap. Limits and consumed counts persist cumulatively across runs and restarts for the configured pilot; explicitly configuring a limit starts its new budget. `maxActions` reserves move/rescue attempts with the durable intent before issuing them, so an unknown provider outcome consumes the cap; it does not cap read flags or local labels. `maxClassifications` limits saved successful classifications, not all provider attempts or synthetic recovery probes. Reaching an attempted excess stops the run with `pilot_complete`. Use preview to disable all provider writes; setting `maxActions:0` in apply mode still permits marking read. The pilot controller requests Pause and verifies processing is disabled on every exit after attempting to start, including failed or uncertain requests. Release validation must separately record a bounded live pilot, measured sustained throughput and a full twenty-four-hour observation; this document does not claim those have completed.

The ordinary `/api/mail/action` endpoint still supports only individual read/unread and star/unstar flags. The automatic processor has its own policy-controlled move path; adding it does not expose arbitrary move, deletion or destination parameters in the browsing API.
